UI/UX CASE STUDY

Fark.com Redesign

OVERVIEW

Fark is a news aggregator and social networking hub created in the late 1990s. This project involved a comprehensive, end-to-end redesign focused on modernizing the platform’s interface and performance without alienating its veteran user base.

MY ROLE

Led the end-to-end redesign of Fark, bridging the gap between a 25-year-old interface and modern user expectations. I led the Conversion Strategy, removing over 50% of signup friction, and redesigned the core site architecture to prioritize instant community engagement. Beyond UX, I spearheaded a full Brand Redesign, establishing a new visual identity and a scalable design system to ensure quality and consistency across screens.

APPROACH

Understanding the Current User Base

Instead of starting with user personas, I researched the community's reaction to the last major redesign in 2007 to understand the "mental models" of long-term users.

INSIGHTS

This archival research revealed that veteran users prioritize speed, density, and efficiency over modern white space. How might we evolve Fark for a new generation without alienating its veteran user base?

Identifying "New" User Pain Points

Six usability tests with new users revealed critical pain points in navigation, content submission, and engagement. These insights informed the redesign's focus on streamlining user flows and reducing friction to foster a more inclusive and engaging community experience.

Confusing Navigation

100% of tested users misidentified the primary navigation path to the forums. What users thought was the "forum" was actually a link to the site the forum was referencing, leading to confusion and frustration.

Confusing Submission Process

80% of users were confused by the "submit a link" process, unclear on the differences between category and topic. Additionally, access to the submission could only be found on the forum page, which would also disappear when new links were posted.

Sign up process took too long

Total time to first engagement exceeded 130 seconds. The lengthy signup process and delayed gratification of account access created a significant barrier to entry, leading to user drop-off.

Difficult Comment Submission

On high-traffic threads, 60% of users had to scroll past 50 comments before reaching the input field. The 'interaction cost' was so high that users abandoned their intent to reply before reaching the input field.

Takeaways

  • Veteran users prioritize speed, density, and efficiency over modern white space.
  • New users struggle with navigation, content submission, and engagement due to unclear UI elements and lengthy signup processes.
  • A comprehensive redesign must balance the needs of both veteran and new users to foster an inclusive and engaging community experience.
Rebrand

Creating a Unified Brand Identity

I needed to address the brand’s outdated and fragmented identity. Fark existed as a collection of URLs and names (Fark, Fark.com, TotalFark, BareFark) rather than a unified community. I stripped the brand back to its core name to build a sole modern platform.

The Post Page

Native Ad System: I designed a ad system to showcase sponsored content while still fitting into the overal design aesthetic.

Submission Process

To reduce confusion I simplified the submission process by replacing the topic input with tags, which are more intuitive for users to understand and use. I also replaced the preview button with a live "preview" feature that allows users to see how their submission will look before they post it, reducing the likelihood of errors and increasing user confidence in the submission process.

The Login/Sign Up Process

Farks sign up process was a major barrier to entry so it required a full revamp.

  • Minimized Interaction: I reduced the total number of required fields by 50%, removing optional input fields like the bio and profile picture, which stalled the sign-up process.
  • Identity Verification: I optimized the Verification Loop by switching to a 6-digit instant code. This transition minimized the 'interaction cost' of creating an account and successfully mitigated the friction of switching apps, which was a primary barrier identified in usability testing.
  • Minimized Interaction: I added an optional "Popular Categories" screen. This provides immediate value and allows new users to start exploring the community’s best content the second they finish the flow.
  • Social & OAuth: By implementing "Sign in with Google/Facebook," I provided a "one-click" alternative for new users, removing the need to create and remember a new set of credentials entirely.

Takeways

Key Learnings

  • Density as a Feature: I learned that "white space" isn't always an improvement.
  • Frictionless Onboarding: By reducing the signup loop from 130 seconds to 60, I saw firsthand how much human potential is lost in technical loops. Small logic changes, like moving to an OTP, have the highest ROI for user growth.
  • Community-Centric Design: Designing for a passionate, long-term user base requires a deep understanding of their values and mental models. Balancing the needs of veteran users with the expectations of new users is crucial for fostering an inclusive and engaging community experience.

Next Steps

  • A/B Testing the Engagement Gate: I plan to test different triggers for the contextual login modal to find the peak conversion point between "Guest" and "Member."
  • Accessibility Audit: Conducting a full audit to ensure the modern nested threading remains fully navigable for screen-reader users and keyboard-only navigation.
  • Edge Cases & Stress Testing: Continuing to identify and address edge cases to ensure the application performs well under unexpected usage scenarios. (Headline Stress Test/Empty State Strategy)
